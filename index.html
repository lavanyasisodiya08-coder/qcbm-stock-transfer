Small-sample research project — NSE small-cap equities

Can a quantum circuit lend a data-poor stock some of a data-rich stock's structure?

A small-cap stock might have a few hundred trading days of usable history — not enough to fit a reliable next-day predictor. This project tests whether generating synthetic training days with a quantum circuit trained on large-cap data helps, compared against bootstrap resampling and a classical Markov chain doing the same job.

The question

Predictors need history to learn from. Large-cap NSE stocks have years of dense trading data; small-caps like LEELAHOTELS or AEGISVOPAK often don't — the sequence is short and thin, and a model trained only on it tends to overfit noise. Four possible fixes to that shortage are compared here, head to head, on the same downstream task: predicting tomorrow's return bucket from a short lookback window.

baseline

No augmentation

Train the predictor on the small-cap's own short history, untouched. The control condition every other method has to beat.

classical

Bootstrap

Resample days from the stock's own history with replacement to pad the training set. Adds volume, no new structure.

classical

Markov cross-transfer

Fit a Markov transition table on pooled large-cap sequences, then generate synthetic days from it to extend the small-cap's training set.

quantum

QCBM cross-transfer

Train a 5-qubit Born-machine circuit on the same pooled large-cap sequences and sample synthetic days from its learned distribution.

How each run works

Every small-cap stock goes through the same pipeline, once per method, so the only thing that changes between rows in the results table is where the extra training days came from.

01

Bucket the history

Daily returns are discretized into labeled buckets, turning price history into a symbol sequence.

02

Time-ordered split

Train / test is split by date, never shuffled — the test window always comes after the training window.

03

Augment the train side only

Synthetic days are appended to the training sequence. Test days are always real, untouched data.

04

Fit the same predictor

A logistic model with a 3-day lookback is fit fresh on each augmented training set — architecture never changes.

05

Score on held-out days

Accuracy and macro-F1 are measured on the same 63 real test days, for every method, on every stock.

reading

On mean accuracy, plain no-augmentation (0.262) edged out every augmentation method, including QCBM cross-transfer (0.238) and bootstrap (0.238) — with classical Markov cross-transfer lowest (0.206). On macro-F1, no-augmentation and bootstrap were close to tied at the top; QCBM and Markov both trailed.

The one place QCBM stood out was consistency: its accuracy std across the two stocks (0.022) was far tighter than bootstrap's (0.067) or no-augmentation's (0.079) — it moved less between the two very different tickers. With n=2 stocks, that's a pattern worth testing further, not a claim.

What this actually shows

This is an honest small-sample result, not a headline. It's worth saying plainly what it does and doesn't support.

holds up

The pipeline runs end to end

Encoding, time-ordered splitting, three augmentation strategies, and a QCBM that actually trains and samples — all verified against real held-out test data, not synthetic placeholders.

holds up

No inflated quantum advantage

On this run, the QCBM did not beat the no-augmentation baseline on accuracy or macro-F1. That's reported as-is rather than reframed as a win.

limit

Two stocks is not a sample

LEELAHOTELS and AEGISVOPAK are one pair. Nothing here generalizes to small-caps broadly until this runs across many more tickers and sectors.

limit

The interesting thread is stability, not accuracy

QCBM's lower variance across two very different stocks is the one signal worth chasing — with a real experiment, not two data points.
